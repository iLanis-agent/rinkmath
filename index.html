The problem

Your yard is not flat, and water knows it.

Every first backyard rink dies the same death: boards set for 4 inches of water, a yard that secretly drops 3, and a low corner that overflows before the shallow end is ankle-deep. Rinkmath does the water-finds-level math first - real depths, liner, lumber, fill time and freeze days - before you buy a single board.

Size your rink

What it computes

The slope truth
Shallow depth plus yard slope equals deep-end depth. If that number beats your boards, the rink spills. Most yards are not flat.
Honest water
Gallons from average depth, and the fill time at a typical 5 gpm garden hose - often a full day, not an afternoon.
Liner and lumber
Liner sized rink plus boards plus overlap, boards from your real perimeter, stakes every ~4 feet, and a typical-cost estimate.
The freeze
Ice growth from your 7-day forecast using the 15 freezing-degree-days-per-inch rule of thumb, plus resurface-flood volume and skater capacity.

The mistakes it prevents

  • The overflow. A 2-inch drop across 40 feet turns a 4-inch plan into a 6-inch reality at the low end. Build boards for the deep end, not the average.
  • The undersized liner. A 20x40 rink with 12-inch boards needs a 25x45 liner once you wrap the boards and leave overlap. A 20x40 tarp fails quietly in week two.
  • The impatient first skate. "It was 20 degrees last night" is not a schedule. Freezing degree-days accumulate; the model shows when the sheet gets there.
  • The surprise water bill. A mid-size rink is 2,500 gallons. Better to know now.
